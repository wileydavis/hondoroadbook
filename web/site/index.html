About fifteen seconds out the cue is spoken. Then short pips rise one octave and come faster as the arrow fills. A two-note chime at the turn. Play one approach — the pips and chime are the app's own sounds, synthesized here the same way; your browser's voice stands in for the app's.

What's a roadbook?

Your route, with turns, notes and hazards placed along the way. Three cues from one:

  • Left onto Dry Creek Road2.4 mi
  • Note last fuel for 90 miles — Town Pump, on the right6.1 mi
  • Danger washout after the cattle guard11 mi

The app's router writes the turns for you; you add the notes and hazards that make it yours. It's an ordinary GPX file — yours to keep, share or edit.

You choose the roads. Hondo helps connect them.

Start with a route, then make it yours. Pull the line onto the roads you want, inspect the terrain and surface, and add the cues you'll want along the way. Save the roadbook and take it with you.

  1. Start with a route. Click two points; pick Fast, Backroads, Dirt, Towing, or a bike mode.
  2. Notice an interesting road. Terrain, land ownership and the surface bar show what it is.
  3. Inspect it. Aerial imagery, what the map knows about the road, fuel, food and camping nearby, the web searched by section.
  4. Pull the route through it. Drag the line onto any road or trail; the cues rewrite themselves.
  5. Add a note. A finding becomes a cue at that spot, link included. Paste a coordinate from anywhere.
  6. Take it to the phone. Send to phone gives a six-letter code; in the app, TRACK → FETCH CODE. Or download the GPX.

Offline, private, and yours to keep.

Navigation runs on your device.Download the maps you need before leaving coverage. The Android app uses those maps and the phone's GPS to follow your route offline — airplane mode is fine. Online features connect only when you choose to download, transfer or share.
Your data isn't the product.We don't collect data to sell it, or to feed anyone else's. No account, no location history, no crash reporting; the app has no Google services in it. The site sets no cookies and runs no analytics script today. The routing service logs a path and a status code, never a position.
Three ways out of the planner, kept distinct.Download GPX gives you a file. Send to phone parks the roadbook under a six-letter code for 24 hours, then it's gone. Publish puts it in the public library. Sharing a planner link shares the points in that link.
Keep the app. Keep your maps. Keep your roadbooks.The app installs from a file on this page, the map packs live on your phone, and a roadbook is an ordinary GPX that any other app can read. Open source, Apache-2.0.
The planner is online.It runs in the browser on the same maps and router as the app, and fetches imagery, elevation, land ownership and web results from their sources as you ask for them. It's the desk; the phone is the ride.
No surprise rerouting.Off the line, the app shows where the route is and how far; a planned route offers REROUTE as a button, a fixed roadbook never changes. Your route stays the one you planned.

Check map coverage

Roads: ½–1 GB per Western state. Terrain: ¼–½ GB more. A 128 GB phone holds the whole West with room to spare. Downloads happen inside the app; the coverage page has the files too.

Why Android only, for now?

Roadbook is handed to you directly: download the file, install it, done. Android allows that; iOS doesn't. An iPhone version would go through the App Store, with an Apple developer account on our side, review on theirs, and their rules about what an app may do in the background. The navigation core is portable and the maps are the same, so it isn't never — if you ride with an iPhone and want this, tell us; the number of those emails decides when.

What does it need?

Android 10 or newer on a 64-bit phone (any from about 2019 on). The phone's own GPS — no SIM or signal needed once the maps are on it. Audio is optional: a Bluetooth helmet headset or earbuds for the spoken cues and pips; the screen works alone. A mount, so it sits where you can glance at it — heading-up, next turn on top.

Does it keep going with the screen off?

Yes. On a bicycle, press the power button and ride by ear: the voice, the pips and the chimes carry on, and so does a recording. A quiet notification shows the next cue with an End navigation button. Some phones (Samsung, Xiaomi) need the app set to Unrestricted in battery settings to let that run.
