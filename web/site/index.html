What's a roadbook?

Your route, with turns, notes and hazards placed along the way. Three cues from one:

  • Left onto Dry Creek Road2.4 mi
  • Note last fuel for 90 miles — Town Pump, on the right6.1 mi
  • Danger washout after the cattle guard11 mi

The router writes the turns for you; you add the notes and hazards that make it yours — at the desk, or from the bars with ADD CUE HERE. It's an ordinary GPX file: yours to keep, share or edit.

You choose the roads. Hondo helps connect them.

Start with a route, then make it yours. Pull the line onto the roads you want, check what they are, add the cues you'll want along the way, and take the roadbook with you.

  1. Shape the route. Click two points and pick a mode — Fast, Backroads, Dirt, Towing, or a bike mode. Then drag the line onto the roads you want; the cues rewrite themselves.
  2. Inspect and annotate. Terrain, surface and land ownership along the way. Aerial imagery, what the map knows, fuel, food and camping nearby. A finding becomes a cue at that spot, link included.
  3. Take it with you. Send to phone gives a six-letter code, and TRACK → FETCH CODE in the app picks it up. Or download the GPX.

How privacy and sharing work
What the app knows about you: nothing
Unlike the apps built around an account, Roadbook isn't in the business of collecting data. No sign-in, no location history, no crash reporting, no Google services, nothing phoned home. This website is an ordinary website: we count how many people visit and what they open, the way any site does, and that's all we know.
The planner is online
It runs in the browser on the same maps and router as the app, and fetches imagery, elevation, land ownership and web results from their sources as you ask for them. Routing requests go to our router; its own log records the request path and a status code, not coordinates. It's the desk; the phone is the ride.
Three ways out of the planner, kept distinct
Download GPX gives you a file. Send to phone parks the roadbook under a six-letter code for 24 hours, then deletes it. Publish puts a public copy of the route and cues in the library, with timestamps, device names and recording metadata removed — the line itself and the words in your cues are public, so write them with that in mind. Sharing a planner link shares the points in that link.
Your cues in other apps
Turns, notes and hazards are stored as GPX waypoints with a type tag. Apps that read GPX will show them as plain waypoints; Roadbook speaks them.
No surprise rerouting
Off the line, the app shows where the route is and how far. A planned route offers REROUTE as a button; a fixed roadbook never changes. Your route stays the one you planned.

Check map coverage

Roads: ½–1 GB per Western state. Terrain: ¼–½ GB more. A 128 GB phone holds the whole West with room to spare. Downloads happen inside the app; the coverage page has the files too.

Why Android only, for now?

The navigation app is Android-only for now. The planner works in any browser on your computer, and exports routes as GPX for whatever you navigate with. Roadbook is handed to you directly — download the file, install it, done — and Android allows that; an iPhone version would go through the App Store, with an Apple developer account on our side and review on theirs. The navigation core is portable and the maps are the same, so it isn't never: if you ride with an iPhone and want this, tell us. The number of those emails decides when.

What does it need?

Android 10 or newer on a 64-bit phone (any from about 2019 on). The phone's own GPS — no SIM or signal needed once the maps are on it. Audio is optional: a Bluetooth helmet headset or earbuds for the spoken cues and pips; the screen works alone. A mount, so it sits where you can glance at it — heading-up, next turn on top.

Does it keep going with the screen off?

Yes. On a bicycle, press the power button and ride by ear: the voice, the pips and the chimes carry on, and so does a recording. A quiet notification shows the next cue with an End navigation button. Some phones (Samsung, Xiaomi) need the app set to Unrestricted in battery settings to let that run.
