Three things it does

Built for a glove: one big button at the bottom opens a sheet of giant buttons. Nothing small to hit at speed.

Real map renders from the Montana pack. The manual walks through every screen.

Plan the ride on a big screen

The planner runs the app's own router on the app's own maps. Click two points, drag the line onto the trails you want, pick Dirt or Backroads, and it writes the cues. Surface, elevation and land ownership along the way; an inspect window with aerial imagery for any spot. Send to phone gives you a six-letter code; in the app, TRACK → FETCH CODE. Nothing is stored unless you publish it to the shared library, which the app can browse too.

Install

  1. On the phone, open roadbook.apk; allow installs from that app; Install.
  2. Allow Precise location. That's the only thing it asks for.
  3. MAPS → DOWNLOAD your states. Ride.

Updates: download again and install over it. Settings and packs stay. Works on GrapheneOS. The app talks to the network only when you press a button that needs it: downloading packs, fetching or publishing a roadbook, browsing the library.

What it needs

Android 10 or newerPixel, Samsung, any phone from about 2019 on. GrapheneOS and other de-Googled builds work; there are no Google services in the app.
64-bit ARMEvery current phone. The voice and map engine ship for arm64 only, so the APK is 40 MB instead of 120.
GPSThe phone's own. No signal or SIM needed once the maps are on it; airplane mode is fine.
Storage½–1 GB per Western state for roads; ¼–½ GB more for terrain. A 128 GB phone holds the whole West with room to spare.
Audio, optionalA Bluetooth helmet headset or earbuds for the spoken cues and pips. The screen works alone.
A mountIt's heading-up with the next turn on top: it wants to be where you can glance at it. We make those.

Why Android only, for now

Roadbook is built to be handed to you directly: download the file from this page, install it, done. No store, no account, no one between you and the app. Android allows that; iOS doesn't. An iPhone version would have to go through the App Store, with an Apple developer account on our side, review on theirs, and their rules about what an app may do in the background.

That isn't a reason never to do it — the navigation core is portable and the maps are the same — but we'd rather make the Android app excellent first, on the phones that let us ship it the way we want to. If you ride with an iPhone and want this, tell us; the number of those emails decides when.
