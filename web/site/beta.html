Roadbook for iPhone: the beta

The iPhone app is the same Roadbook as Android — the same router, the same maps, the same planner — and it is in open beta while it earns its place on the App Store. Anyone with an iPhone can ride it now through Apple's TestFlight. Free, no account with us, no tracking; the privacy page is short and applies in full.

What "beta" means here

It works: people are planning and riding with it. It is also changing weekly, and a few things are rough at the edges. You get every new build automatically, and your settings, maps and files stay through updates. If something is wrong, say so — that is the point of the beta.

Install it

  1. On the iPhone, install TestFlight from the App Store. It is Apple's own app for pre-release software; it is free and takes a minute.
  2. Open the join link below on the iPhone. TestFlight opens, says Roadbook; tap Accept, then Install.
  3. Open Roadbook. Allow Precise location (the GPS is how it follows you) and, if you want the next cue on the lock screen with the screen off, notifications.
  4. ≡ → MAPS → DOWNLOAD your states or provinces at home on wifi. Then ride. The guide shows every job step by step.

Good to know

  • Updates arrive through TestFlight on their own (or tap Update in TestFlight). A beta build expires 90 days after it was uploaded; there is always a newer one by then.
  • Feedback: take a screenshot inside Roadbook and choose Share Beta Feedback — it reaches us with the build and device attached — or write to wiley@machinenusa.com. The most useful reports say what you expected and what happened instead.
  • Sound goes to a helmet comm or headphones over Bluetooth, ducking music while a cue plays. If you hear nothing, SOUND ON in the menu says where the audio is going.
  • Maps you downloaded before the places index was added (late October 2026) do not show fuel, camping and water on the map until downloaded again once.
  • Android riders get the app from the home page as a plain download — no beta programme needed.

Roadbook is a riding aid, not a substitute for your eyes, your judgment or the law. The map is made by volunteers and can be wrong; you ride at your own risk. The terms say so at length. Made by Hondo Garage, Manhattan, Montana.
